1. Introduction 
Azienda Elettrica Ticinese, AET, was established in 1958 with the mission of producing, transmitting 
and commercializing electricity. T oday, AET is the leading electricity wholesaler in the Canton of 
Ticino and is  one of the key players in the cantonal energy transition. 
While the Swiss market remains its main reference market, AET also operates in several European 
markets and maintains bilateral and over-the counter (OTC) energy and commercial relationships 
with leading operators. AET is recognized as a reliable and qualified counterparty on the main 
European power exchanges. 
Participation in power markets requires a high level of expertise and knowledge  in front -office 
activities such as  screen trading,  middle -office activities such as analysis, and in back -office 
activities.  
However, these capabilities alone are insufficient: to effectively assess market opportunities and 
positions, analysis and trading activities require price curves  that are aligned with the granularity of 
traded products and operational processes. 
This requirement has become particularly relevant with the transition of the European day -ahead 
market to quarter-hourly granularity. From 1st  October 2025 onwards,  prices that were previously 
determined mainly on an hourly basis, will be  formed for 15 -minute intervals. However, many 
forecasts, historical series and long -term curves remain available only at hourly granularity. 
Therefore, a method is needed to reconstruct, for each hour, a realistic quarter-hourly profile for each 
hour while preserving the original hourly average price. 
Why the hourly price is not enough: simply replicating the hourly price over the four quarter-hours is 
not sufficient because, even though the  average of the values is mathematically equivalent to the 
hourly price, it does not capture the volatile intra-hour price dynamics. . Observed data often exhibits 
recurring patterns, such as sawtooth profiles, which are important for position valuation, trading, and 
the training of 15-minute models.

2. Definitions 
Definitions Description 
CET/CEST Central European Time / Central European Summer Time. The Central European 
time zone, is equal to UTC+1 under standard time (CET) and UTC+2 under daylight 
saving time (CEST). 
DST Daylight Saving Time. A seasonal time regime in which clocks are advanced by 
one hour compared with standard time. In Europe it is in force from the last 
Sunday in March to the last Sunday in October, with the clock change taking 
place at 01:00 UTC. 
ENTSO-E European Network of Transmission System Operators for Electricity. It is a 
European association of transmission system operators (TSOs), including 
Swissgrid for Switzerland. Its responsibilities include operating the Transparency 
Platform, which publishes market and system data such as prices, load, 
generation, and cross-border flows. 
MAE Mean Absolute Error. An accuracy indicator equal to the mean of the absolute 
differences between estimated and observed values: MAE = (1/n) · Σ |pᵢ − p̂ᵢ|. It is 
expressed in the same unit as the measured quantity ( e.g. EUR/MWh) and, unlike 
other indicators, it does not amplify the weight of larger errors. 
OTC Over-the-Counter. Bilateral trading outside of regulated exchanges, concluded 
directly between counterparties or through brokers. Contracts may be 
standardized or customized, and their terms are agreed between the parties. 
SDAC Single Day-Ahead Coupling. European single day-ahead market coupling 
mechanism that simultaneously determines prices and cross-border electricity 
exchanges in participating zones simultaneously, taking available transmission 
capacities into account. Tt has operated at 15-minute granularity since 1 October 
2025. 
UTC Coordinated Universal Time. An international reference time scale that has not 
subject to changes between standard time and daylight saving time. For this 
reason, it is commonly used for recording and exchanging time series, as it 
ensures a continuous and unambiguous sequence of intervals. 
3. Day-ahead market context 
In the day -ahead market, the spot price of electricity is determined one day before delivery.  This 
means that electricity is actually bought and sold in advance. Market participants submit buy and 
sell orders for the various delivery (intervals) periods of the next day in a so-called day-ahead auction 
. The SDAC algorithm then uses  a single market -clearing process to match supply and demand , 
taking into account capacity constraints between market areas.  
This determines a single market price for each market area and each delivery interval. All participants 
in the same area therefore refer to the same price for that specific interval. The ordered sequence of 
these prices over the day constitutes the daily price curve.

4. Information available in the challenge 
The day -ahead auction jointly determines the prices for each delivery interval for the next day. 
Participants will therefore receive all 24 hourly prices for each zone and evaluation date together, so 
they do not need to forecast subsequent hourly prices. Participants may process each hour 
separately or the entire curve, using the method they consider most appropriate. During the training 
process, only the data included in the official data package provided by organizers may be used. 
5. Challenge objective 
Participants must develop a model that, for each market area and day, transforms a curve composed 
of 24 hourly prices into 96 15-minute prices, i.e. four values for each hour.

To keep the dataset uniform, only days representable by 24 hours and 96 15-minute intervals will be 
included. Days affected by the daylight-saving-time transition, which result in 23 or 25 hours, will be

Core constraint: for each hour, the average of the four reconstructed quarter -hourly prices must 
equal the reference hourly price. The model learns the shape within the hour, not a new hourly level.

6. Target definition 
The official target comprises the four observed 15 -minute prices for each hour. The model must 
produce four values , the average of which equals the provided hourly price. There is n o specific 
decomposition or parameterization prescribed: teams are free to choose how to represent and 
estimate the intra-hour profile. 
Constraint for each hour h:  (1/4) * sum[q=1..4] P_hat(h,q) = H(h) 
In the formula, H(h) denotes the provided hourly price and P_hat(h,q) the predicted price for quarter-
hour q. The constraint defines the required output, but not the method used to obtain it. 
7. Data availability 
The official training set comprises a single historical file containing 15-minute day-ahead prices for 
the following four zones, identified as Zone 0, Zone 1, Zone 2 and Zone 3. There are  363 valid days 
available for each zone, equating to a total of 139,392 quarter-hourly prices. The hourly training curve 
can be calculated as the arithmetic mean of the four prices within the same hour. 
Data source and processing: the day-ahead prices used for the challenge originate from the ENTSO-
E Transparency Platform. AET subsequently selected, processed and reorganized the data for the 
purposes of the challenge .  In particular, market areas were distributed to participants using 
anonymized identifiers (ZONE_0, ZONE_1, ZONE_2, ZONE_3). References to ENTSO -E exclusively 
identify the source of publication of the data and do not imply sponsorship, approval or endorsement 
of the challenge by ENTSO-E. 
Time alignment: daily curves group together intervals belonging to the same delivery date. Source 
timestamps are normalized to CET/CEST market time and observations are not solely aggregated on 
the basis of the UTC date.

Dataset design choice: a single quarter -hourly file is sufficient for training, from which the hourly

Data design principle 
Load, generation, and weather forecasts are not provided to the participants. The reason for this is 
that the solution must also be applicable to various types of forward curve and to long-term horizons 
for which thesevariables/forecasts are simply  not available. Teams are free to choose variables 
derived solely from the permitted data and the 15-min the reconstruction method.  
Use of historical data during inference.  
During development, participants may only use the official training set for  training, calibration and 
hyperparameter selection . However, as part of its inference logic, the solution may include the use 
of lagged variables or other features derived from quarter -hourly history prior to the date to be 
reconstructed. During evaluation, history_15min.csv may therefore also include observations after 
the official training period and  before the evaluated delivery date. These observations can only be 
used as inputs to the frozen pipeline according to rules established before the freezing. They cannot 
be used to retrain the model, recalibrate parameters, modify hyperparameters or adapt the 
methodology. 
For instance, when evaluating the delivery date of 19 October 2026, the pipeline may use the quarter-
hourly prices of earlier dates ,   including those after 30 September 2026, as lags, provided this  is  
accounted for  in the submitted pipeline and does not require updating the model.

8. Input rules 
Availability rule 
For each zone and evaluated date, the pipeline will receive  the 24 hourly prices for the day and a 
history of 15-minute prices from earlier dates. Teams may use the entire hourly curve and construct 
lagged variables from the history. Historical data provided after the freeze, even if more recent than 
the training set, may feed only inference rules already fixed in the submitted code: t hey may not be 
used to retrain the model, calibrate parameters or modify the rules. External sources and quarter -
hourly prices for the day being reconstructed are prohibited. 
Pretrained models and use of external code 
Pretrained models, external weights, transfer learning, embeddings or components trained on data 
not included in the official package are not allowed. Standard libraries, statistical methods, 
traditional Machine Learning  models and code written by participants are allowed, provided that 
training, validation, calibration and hyperparameter selection depend exclusively on the official 
challenge training set. 
Simple, robust, interpretable and easily verifiable solutions will be viewed positively by the jury. 
Model complexity is not in itself a merit and must be justified by the benefits obtained. 
9. Pipeline output and execution 
During the evaluation stage, p articipants will not directly compute predictions themselves for the 
secret evaluation day(s). The final evaluation will be carried out by the jury by using the code, model 
and artifacts submitted by the teams. 
The solution must  therefore include a complete, documented and easily executable inference 
pipeline that, for each evaluated date, receives test_hourly.csv and history_15min.csv provided by 
the organizers as input and automatically produces the required quarter -hourly predictions. 
Execution must be possible without code modifications, without network access and without 
undocumented manual intervention. Teams must clearly specify the command, script or entry point 
required to start inference. 
The pipeline must produce a file containing one price prediction for each delivery_date, market_id,

Quantitative evaluation 
The quantitative metric is the Mean Absolute Error (MAE), i.e. the mean absolute difference between 
estimated and observed 15 -minute prices, expressed in EUR/MWh. The MAE is first calculated for 
each day and zone, then averaged across days for each zone. The official score is the simple average 
of the MAEs of the four zones: each has a 25% weight and a lower value is better. 
MAE_day = mean over 96 quarter-hours |P_hat(d,h,q) - P_obs(d,h,q)| 
MAE_zone = mean over days MAE_day 
Score = mean over zones MAE_zone 
The quantitative score will be calculated exclusively by the organizers by running the submitted 
solution on the secret evaluation dataset. 
Diagnostics published together with the score: 
− error by zone and by quarter-hour position within the hour; 
− error distribution and analysis of the highest-error cases; 
− compliance with the constraint that keeps the hourly average unchanged; 
− comparison with the flat baseline, which replicates the hourly price over the four quarter -
hours. 
Qualitative evaluation 
The quantitative ranking based on MAE is one component of the final evaluation and does not alone 
determine the winner. The jury will also qualitatively  assess the simplicity of the approach, its 
robustness, its interpretability,the clarity of the methodology and the reproducibility of the pipeline. 
The extent to which any introduced complexity is actually justified by the results obtained will also 
be considered. 
The qualitative evaluation will be performed on the submitted solution, code and methodology 
document . A simpler approach does not automatically receive a better evaluation, but unnecessary 
or difficult-to-verify complexity may be penalized relative to a solution with comparable predictive 
quality that is more transparent.

13. Essential FAQ 
Does the challenge also involve forecasting hourly prices? 
No, the hourly curve is an input that is already available. The task is to reconstruct its 15 -minute 
shape exclusively. 
Does the model have to process each hour separately? 
No,  all 24 hourly prices for the same day are provided together , so the team can model individual 
hours or the entire curve. They may also use the quarter-hourly history made available to the pipeline 
by the jury, but this is always limited to earlier dates and does not include the 15-minute prices for 
the day (being predicted) in question. 
Is it possible to train a single model for all zones? 
Yes., both a single model for all zones, using “market_id” to distinguish them, and separate models 
are permitted. The average of the MAEs of the four zones gives each zone the same weight in the 
official score. 
Are additional data such as demand, generation or weather provided? 
No, the official package only includes the 15-minute training prices.  During the evaluation, the jury 
will provide the hourly prices for the days to be reconstructed , as well as the prior quarter-hourly 
history. Load, generation and weather data are not provided, and variables may not be obtained from 
external sources. 
Are pretrained models allowed? 
No. To ensure comparability and reproducibility,  particpants must exclusively use the official 
challenge training set and code that has not been trained on external series   for training, validation, 
calibration and hyperparameter selection.
